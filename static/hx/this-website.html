This Website

I started off making this website at a time in my life when I was drifting anti-AI. I thought to myself that it not only would be more satisfying to be able to say that I created my own HTTP server for it in C, wrote all of the backend logic in C, and then also wrote all of the the javascript, the HTML, the CSS, it would also lead to a better website and a better use of my time. I doubted AI's ability to create what I really wanted.

This was after I had spent more than a year creating a company that I woudln't have been able to create without Claude Code. I vibe-coded, pair programmed, agentic coded -- whatever you want to call it -- nearly every part of the products I made for that company. I enjoyed doing it too; I entered a real flow state at times.

That pro-AI phase ended as I realized that there was little to no demand for the products I had made; that, or I was so bad at selling the second product door-to-door that I had made a mistake even creating it in the first place. I learned the hard way that, in business, the thrill of creation was not a substitute for a good idea.

So, I wrote the HTTP server in C. I used AI to show me the outline of how to use a socket, epub, and eventfd, and I wrote every character of code with my own fingers, no copying and pasting. I also really studied the things I was creating, and the tools and the process of debugging them as well as C code. I really learned to love command line tools like gdb, curl, and ss, as well as the Linux's use of file descriptors. The whole experience was incredibly satisfying. And then I start to work on CSS, and it all came to a halt.

I could figure out CSS, I was putting in the time and making progress. But, I am an economist by training, attempting to be a data scientist by trade, and unemployed. The likelihood of CSS every really being the difference betweeen me getting a job or not, or me developing the right model or not, is infinitesimal. So, after I had spent two afternoons trying to get a bit of ASCII art to subtly fade in and move in the background of my homepage, I began to have my doubts about the romantic notion of "doing it all myself''.

Then of course there is the ever-present advance of the field of AI, of its products and its capabilities. Yes, AI might not be able to solve certain technical problems now, but surely it will be able to later, to some degree. I am confronted with the question of how much of me valuing my own technical understanding and output is vanity, and how much is necessity?

This question begs another: what, in general, should I do with the productive side of my life? In some sense, this website has become a maditation on that subject. As of September 5th, 2026, most of my idle thoughts have as well.

There is now, currently and perhaps subject to change, no doubt in my mind that AI can help me create what I really want, that it can help create a world that all of us want. In fact this is a matter of belief: humanity's striving for technological prowess is redeemed by the inherently benevolent spirit of that striving. There is a malevolent spirit too, but it does not run rampant. If there is one thing that dominates nearly everything in my productive life, it's the thought of how to get the best possibly future for myself and others in the age of AI.

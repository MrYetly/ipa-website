What's the point of working on anything anymore? Contrary to the zeitgeist, I am not interested in this question from teh point of view of a doomer. It is naive to think that we can stop AI becoming whatever it will become. But, I also spent most of the past year and a half starting a company that I wouldn't have been able to start without AI. Call it vibe-coding, call it AI pair programming, I was doing it, a lot. And while I did often get into a flow state, I would just as often enter a prompt and turn my brain off. It straightup did not feel good. And so now I have come to the point where I believe there must be a better way to do things. How can we keep that deeply human feeling of being in a flow state while we are working, while also taking advantage of the often breathtaking abilities of AI.

It seems like the best way to do this is to minimize teh amount of time you are waiting for an AI to do something. This requires that you maximize its independence, and focus only on what you should be doing.

Then of course the question becomes, what should you be focusing on? The elephant in the room here is that AI already is better than you at many different tasks, and may one day be better than you at everything. It is clear to me that AIs still struggle to create and execute long term visions: this is why an AI isn't writing the next great novel every minute. But crafting a vision has never been something that takes up the majority of the time of any work.

Should human's simply become vision machines, churning out ideas for the long run? That doesn't seem likely.

It is obvious that AI has created an even greater premium on human knowledge and ability. If Steve Jobs wanted to create the bicyle for the mind, AI has given it a stable of Ferraris that may or may not be able to drive themselves.
